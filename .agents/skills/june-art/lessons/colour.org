* Lessons for fixing colours
** Prefer a pixel recolour to a generation
A colour fix should change colour and nothing else. =june-art run recolor= shifts hue and saturation for pixels matching a range inside a region. It keeps each pixel's value, so shading survives, and never touches alpha. Evidence: =gentle-standing-hands-clasped='s silver buckle became gold (2026-11-08, 344 pixels in a 35x28 region), alpha byte-identical.

** The region is the mask
A tight box plus a saturation and value range isolated a metal buckle from a dark navy belt. A few edge pixels that blend into a neighbour can keep a faint tint; check at 8x. Big regions are harder: her blouse and skin are close in hue, so a blouse fix needs a better mask than a box.

** Measure a target the way the tool measures art
Palette swatches are single spots, and matching a pose's median to one gives the wrong colour. Blouse swatch #C2817C turned tall poses mauve and a portrait bubblegum in the 2026-11-08 pilot. Canon's blouse measured with the same selection is #D7756A, close to what the tall poses already were. Use =recolor --match=, which keeps each pixel's offset from the typical colour so folds and shadows survive.

** Fix the source, then re-ingest
Recolour the source RGBA, ingest it with the pose's measured face, mouth and gestures, and compare the manifest entry with the old one. It should differ only in what you meant to change. Type gesture names exactly as the sidecar has them.

** Match mode can change brightness
The value-preservation rule above applies to hue-and-saturation shifts, not =--match=. Match mode shifts value toward the target median too, while preserving pixel offsets until clipping. In the 2026-11-08 library run, blouse matching changed brightness as well as hue. Judge the result against canon; a target median alone is not acceptance.

** Colour ranges do not protect skin or hair
The 56-pose run on 2026-11-08 selected wrist shadows, finger edges and hair highlights with the blouse range. =curious-head-tilt= visibly changed hair tips. Inspect a binary before/after difference mask as well as the comparison, and revert an item when its boundary is uncertain. Do not rely on the runbook's claim that colour excludes skin and hair.

** Reject threshold patchwork
A region can cover the whole blouse while its colour range selects only parts. =determined-ready-front= and =pout-reluctant-front= developed speckles and hard colour boundaries in the first 2026-11-08 run. Widening the region cannot fix a hue-range boundary inside the cloth; a garment mask with no hue range can.

** Inspect the whole boot at native size
Starting below a boot's top can leave a horizontal colour join. The first 2026-11-08 =determined-hands-on-hips= attempt left matching straight joins on both shafts, obvious in a native crop but easy to miss on a contact sheet. A =boots:2= mask covers the whole shaft and removed the join.

** Check tilted eyes against the source
A face-sized eye region can select green fringe outside the irises. A tight rectangle can also cut a tilted iris: the first =curious-standing-lean-front= region missed part of her left iris. A source-grid polygon covered both irises without the lower fringe. Check a native face crop after correction, not just the changed-pixel count.

** Segment garments rather than outlining them
Hand regions and colour ranges were the main cause of failure: the first 56-pose run kept only 17 blouse and 3 skirt steps out of the whole library, with no boots. SAM3 masks on eight hard poses on 2026-11-08 followed the blouse around hands and hair and both boots whole. The skirt mask can include the belt, so the target subtracts ="belt"=.

** SAM3 finds what you ask for, even when it is absent
="boots:2"= on =working-typing-laptop=, which shows no boots, returned a 1.6% mask and recoloured something that was not boots. List a garment only for a pose that shows it.

** Match eye brightness to a target that looks right
The first eye target, =#87B649=, pulled dim irises up to its brightness and Jon called them radioactive. =keepValue= on the darker =#69732A= still left irises drawn bright: run 3's =thoughtful-pen= and =curious-head-tilt= went from emerald to yellow-lime. Run 4 dropped =keepValue=, so every iris lands on the reference's darkness, and they matched =gentle-offering-mug-front=. Pick the eye target from a pose that looks right, then judge it on the brightest irises as well as the dim ones.

** Near-neutral cloth needs its hue and saturation flattened
The =warm-hands-clasped= skirt was blue-black and mauve-grey. Keeping each pixel's hue offset spread those into purple and olive with a green rim, which =hueKeep: 0= fixed. Run 3 then lost 26 of 55 skirts because saturation was still multiplied per pixel, up to 3x, and tan edge highlights turned red. =satKeep: 0= gives every pixel the target saturation and leaves only the drawn shading. All 55 skirts passed in run 4, including =pout-reluctant-front='s mottled shadows.

** A dim pose needs its brightness moved as well
With =keepValue=, =wry-mug-toast='s blouse went from =#916661= to =#91524B=, correctly coloured and much darker than the rest. Overriding that step with =keepValue: false= and =strength: 0.6= gave =#BB7269=, in family with the others and with its folds intact.

** A clean mask does not prove the colour is good
Run 3's red rims came from solid, correct masks. Inspect the cloth and its outlines at native size. When many poses fail the same way, fix the transform instead of dropping them one at a time.

** Coverage cannot approve a garment mask
=idle-listening-earcup-front='s larger mask included tights in the pleat openings. Belt subtraction does not prove that everything left is skirt. Review the waistband, pleats and tights separately.

** A near-empty mask can come from the subtraction
=serious-calm-report-front='s skirt mask covered 0.01% with every prompt tried, in run 3 and in run 4. The pose has no belt, only a wide waistband, so ="belt"= matched the skirt and subtracting it erased the mask. A step with ="subtract": []= gave 8.7%. When coverage is near zero, remove the subtraction before rewording the prompt.

** Apply brightness overrides pose by pose
Run 3 found eight more dim blouses that needed the existing =keepValue: false=, =strength: 0.6= override, including =idle-headset-adjust-front=, =wry-knowing-smile-front= and =pout-reluctant-front=. Their reviewed results kept folds without staying brown. Keep the override local; the other 47 blouses did not need it.

** Check finished alpha as well as source alpha
Run 3's first =curious-listening-fresh-m= WebP differed from production in 1,343 alpha pixels, despite exact source alpha. Original-source and corrected-source replays both matched production alpha. Rerunning the same approved colour plan produced identical source RGBA and a finished WebP with exact production alpha; PNG file hashes differed. The initial mismatch did not reproduce, so its cause remains unknown. Verify every finished WebP instead of inferring its alpha from the source.

** Keep each small command's report before running the next
=run masks= replaces =SET/colour-masks.json=, and =colours= replaces =SET/colour-report.json= with the current plan's results. The eleven one-pose plans on 2026-11-09 needed separate saved reports to retain all 33 garment coverages and 41 colour steps. Save them in each pose's host-local run directory; a final top-level report covers only the last pose.

** Check that a mask's shape matches the pose
When nails was re-staged with seed 115501 on 2026-11-09, =run masks --force= segmented a cached =RUN/flat/NAME.png= of the old take. The garment coverages matched the old take to four decimals, and the masks outlined the old blouse and boots. The runner now flattens the source every time. Coverage alone would not have caught it: lay each mask beside the pose and compare outlines.

** A wrong-metal buckle needs a mask and flat colour
=skeptical-standing-raised-eyebrow='s silver buckle came out pale champagne with the plain =belt-hardware= target, because near-grey metal hits the saturation cap. A step with ="segment": "belt buckle"=, =hueKeep: 0= and =satKeep: 0= found it without coordinates and gave the library's gold (2026-11-09).
