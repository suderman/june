#+TITLE: What June does

What June does beyond answering you, and how each part works. The keys that switch these on are in [[file:configuration.org][configuration]].

* The moment
June's prompt ends with a =now= section: the weekday, the rough hour, when the sun next sets or rises, today's and tomorrow's weather, today's and tomorrow's calendar entries, and which zone each configured person is in. She uses it to judge, not to narrate. Home Assistant is polled in the background, so a reply never waits on it, and lines it cannot fill are left out. The section changes about once an hour, so the prompt cache mostly holds; the =clock= tool gives the exact time.

Until the user and June have spoken on a given day, a =greeting= section asks her to open her first words with a greeting for the time of day, whether she answers the user or reaches out first, and to answer in kind when the user greeted her. The day starts at =rhythm.wake=, so a chat past midnight still counts as the same day. Contact is a message the user wrote or a message June sent, not her own task inputs or a quiet heartbeat; the section is never a reason to break a quiet heartbeat. It lives in =src/greeting.ts=.

* Heartbeat
Every =heartbeat.everyMinutes= between =wake= and =quiet=, June takes a private look at the moment in the main conversation, with her standing instructions from =HEARTBEAT.org=, what changed in her =now= section since the last look, and whether the user is chatting. She answers =QUIET= or a message. The user never sees the look itself: the daemon's view hides its input, tools, and quiet answers, showing only a real message. A heartbeat waits while June is busy, and a receipt in the state directory keeps a restart from repeating one. June updates =HEARTBEAT.org= when told what to watch for.

* Loose ends
With =looseEnds= on, once a day from =looseEnds.at= until =rhythm.quiet=, June takes one more private look, shaped like a heartbeat: she reads the day's conversation and her journal entries for today for at most a few loose ends that would leave a gap when the nightly reflection closes the day, such as a conversation the user started without saying how it ended, or a family outing she never heard the end of. It is not a task audit. She asks in one natural message or answers =QUIET=; a day the user sent her no message asks no model at all, even if she wrote in the journal herself. When the user answers, she writes it in the day's journal as she would anything they share, and the reflection reads both the journal and the conversation, so an answer fills the gap and silence leaves it open: the reflection is told never to guess an outcome. Nothing waits for an answer. A receipt in the state directory, =loose-ends.json=, makes it once a day across restarts; a look missed until after =quiet= is skipped. The user's =HEARTBEAT.org= instructions apply to it too.

With =mail= on, each look also lists the mail that arrived since the last one, sorted before June sees it. Messages from people come first, marked when the user has written to them before; automated senders (no-reply and alert addresses, mail domains such as =updates.=, auto-replies) follow in one line each; newsletters and list mail (=List-Unsubscribe=, =List-Id=, bulk =Precedence=) are only counted; the user's own messages are left out. She sees senders and subjects only, framed as outside text, and reads a message with notmuch when it may matter.

* Syncthing conflicts
The user's Org tree syncs between hosts, and Syncthing leaves =NAME.sync-conflict-DATE-TIME-DEVICE.EXT= copies when two of them change a file at once. The =org-conflicts= skill in =skills/= resolves them for June and every agent: its =org-conflict= command lists copies under the Org root, retires those identical to the live file, editor locks and temporary files, and Org copies whose every line the live file already has, and shows the lines only a diverged Org copy holds. The skill's judgment covers the rest: merge what the copy alone holds, then retire it. Copies of other files are listed and left for the user. June's writers still refuse a file with a copy beside it and point her at the skill.

With =conflicts= on, once a day from =conflicts.at= until =rhythm.quiet=, the daemon runs the sweep over =org.root=, and when Org copies are left for judgment June gets a private look, shaped like a heartbeat, listing them; she resolves them with the skill or hands them to a working context, and answers =QUIET= unless something needs the user. A receipt, =conflicts.json=, makes it once a day across restarts. Run it on one host only, the one June's daemon runs on, so Syncthing sees a single writer.

* Watching dispatched work
June follows the tasks she queues herself. With =watch= on, she also hears about every other task org-dispatch runs on =dispatch.hosts=, such as ones the user set to =AUTO=: once a minute, with no model call, June reads the dispatcher's run records on each host (=~/.local/state/org-dispatch=, over SSH for other hosts), checks each live run's Org state and its agent's Herdr status, and notes when a run reaches =EVAL=, =HOLD=, or =DONE=, or its agent is blocked. Pending news waits for waking hours and at least =gapMinutes= since the last such notice, then reaches her as one =[watch]= input, and she tells the user what came of it. She leaves those tasks' states alone. The first look only learns what is already there.

* Steering a worker
When the user asks June to send directions or a question to an existing worker, she inspects its Herdr pane and sends the follow-up in the same session, then reads its output to relay the response. This works on the daemon host; other hosts remain read-only. It does not resume a closed session or change an Org task's state. Approval and question dialogs need the user, and Herdr rejects recognized blocked dialogs without typing. A successful submission is not proof the worker has started or finished. Prompts are not replayed after a restart; after an error, June checks the pane before retrying because delivery may already have happened.

* Shell and files
June has Pi Durable's =bash=, =read=, =edit=, and =write= tools, running on the daemon host as the user. Commands stop after 120 seconds unless given a longer =timeout=, and =/stop= kills them. A call interrupted by a restart comes back as interrupted rather than running again. =edit= fails when the text it replaces has changed. The tools grant capability; =AGENTS.md= sets what she does herself and what needs approval.

* Chat, focus, work, and patter
- Chat (=model.chat=) is everyday talk. Focus (=model.focus=) is careful work; the =focus= tool switches to it and back, and a restart or =/new= starts relaxed.
- =work= hands a self-contained brief to a background child conversation with her tools and identity. The call returns at once, so the user can keep talking; only the final report comes back, as a =[work]= follow-up. A restart resumes the same brief without running it twice.
- Patter (=model.patter=) keeps the user company during a long wait with short playful lines that never claim results or promise anything. Lines are display-only: no model context, compaction, or memory review contains them.

* Memory and journal
- =SOUL.org= is June's identity.
- =SELF.org= is June's own account of who she has become from living with the user, where =SOUL.org= is who the user made her: running jokes, names that stuck, opinions she has formed, what she is proud of, how she and the user feel lately, and how her moods trended. Facts about the user stay in =MEMORY.org=. The week's reflection, right after its "Week's end" digest, revises it rather than rewriting it, and it may end with a "Proposals for SOUL" heading of changes for the user to apply; June mentions a new one once and never applies it herself. Her prompt shows it directly after =SOUL.org=, so the prompt runs from solid to fleeting: =SOUL.org=, =SELF.org=, then =MOOD.org= near the end. Nothing else writes it. It needs =week= in =reflection.periods= (the default), and is first written by the first week that closes with it deployed.
- =MEMORY.org= is a page or two of lasting facts about the user's life and how to work with them. June updates it when told something lasting, and compaction and the nightly reflection review it.
- =MOOD.org= is how June woke up feeling: a short first-person paragraph and a few lingering threads, written by the nightly reflection with =SOUL.org= in view and replaced whole each night. It starts from the previous night's mood, so moods shift rather than reset, and drifts back toward her default on a quiet day. Her prompt shows it just before the =now= section, saying the day's conversation outweighs it and that it colours her voice, never facts or task status. Nothing else writes it; she never edits it herself. It appears after the first night, and a day without journal entries leaves it as it was.
- =JOURNAL.org= is June's own dated journal, written with the =journal= tool and read on demand. It is not in her prompt, apart from the last few "Day's end" entries and the latest week's and month's digests.
- =org.people= (=~/org/people.org= by default) is the user's rolodex, which June keeps: one heading per person (pets the household cares about included) with =:WHO:= (who they are to the user), =:EMAIL:=, =:PHONE:=, =:ADDRESS:=, and =:BIRTHDAY:= properties, a summary paragraph of who they are now, and dated notes. She adds new people and contact details as they come up in conversation, mail, and Org; the nightly reflection carries highlights from the day (what they said or did, interests, personality, milestones) into their notes and refreshes their summary, with a lower bar for the people closest to the user. Someone the rolodex lacks goes under a =New= heading. She puts contact details in the address book too, which she reads and edits with khard. The heartbeat's mail list names senders from both, and a sender either one knows by name counts as a person.
- Each night, in the sleep hours, once the user has gone quiet, one request rewrites the day just gone with the whole day in view, closes it with a "Day's end" entry (ending with a sentence on how June felt that day), carries lasting facts into =MEMORY.org=, rewrites =MOOD.org= (with bitcoin's day as one more input when =reflection.bitcoin= is on), and adds what it learned about people to the rolodex. The replaced text is kept under =reflection/= in the state directory.
- On the last day of a week (Sunday), month, quarter, or year, a longer pass follows the nightly one and writes a digest: "Week's end" under the Sunday, "Month's end" and "Quarter's end" under the month, "Year's end" under the year. A week reads its days in full, a month its days' endings and weeks, and a quarter or year its months' digests. The week's pass also revises =SELF.org= in the same request, keeping the page it replaces under =reflection/=. A night that is missed catches up on the next one.
- Before compaction, one request produces the working summary and a memory review. A failed review or save skips compaction and keeps the old context. Saves are atomic renames checked against the reviewed text and refuse Syncthing conflict copies; this is not a cross-host lock.

* Org tasks
June reads Org with ripgrep and writes only through =org-edit= and the dispatcher. With =dispatch.hosts= set she can:

- find or create a project file under =~/org/work= (=org_projects=, =org_create_project=);
- queue a new AUTO task, now or for a later start written with it, and nudge the dispatcher (=org_queue_task=, =org_dispatch_now=), then follow it and report its transitions;
- queue a plan of connected tasks, each waiting on its prerequisites through =:DEPENDS:=, and follow every one (=org_queue_plan=);
- stop a running local dispatcher task when the user asks, park it at HOLD before interrupting its exact worker, and confirm interruption before any separately authorized reassignment (=org_stop_task=);
- resume an existing TODO, EVAL, or HOLD task when the user asks, optionally adding prerequisites it waits on; a stopped run gets a fresh worker, and unconfirmed stops cannot resume (=org_resume_task=);
- remove selected prerequisites when the user asks, pausing AUTO at HOLD before a separate authorized resume (=org_remove_dependencies=);
- follow any task the user names, with a progress update every few minutes while it is PROG (=org_follow_task=);
- with =dispatch.improve= set, hand a gap the user hit in what she can do to a coding agent, with their words and the =june transcript= command for that stretch of conversation (=june_improve=);
- close a task she created once its result is reviewed (=org_complete_task=).

She never writes =asana.org=, =inbox.org=, archives, or conflict copies. The dispatcher, =org-edit=, =herdr-link=, their prompts, and the =project-org-tasks=, =org-triage=, and =org-conflicts= skills live in =org/= and =skills/=; see [[file:../org/README.org][org/README.org]].
